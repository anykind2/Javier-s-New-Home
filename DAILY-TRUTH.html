02 — Borrowed words

Six ideas I keep in my pocket.

Small words from elsewhere that made my own days easier to carry.

06 concepts, no ownership
01 生き甲斐

IKIGAI

The reason you wake up each morning excited to do something. When you have a purpose, you won’t feel lazy or unmotivated.

02 改善

KAIZEN

Focus on small improvements every day. Don’t wait for the big leap — try to be 1% better today than you were yesterday.

03 初心

SHOSHIN

In the beginner’s mind there are many possibilities; in the expert’s mind there are few. Stay willing to be bad at it.

04 腹八分

HARA HACHI BU

Stop eating when you’re about 80% full. Leave a little room — for food, for rest, for the unexpected.

05 森林浴

SHINRIN-YOKU

“Forest bathing.” Spend more time with nature. Walk without a destination and let your shoulders drop.

06 我慢

Patience and perseverance when things get hard. Not everything difficult is an emergency.

03 — The back of receipts

Things worth writing down.

Some of these are mine. Some are borrowed. All of them have been tested by a bad week.

The two-day rule

Miss once. Never twice.

“You can miss one day if you must. But never two days in a row.”

Say you’ve started exercising every day, then something happens and you can’t train for a day. That’s fine. But if you miss two days in a row, not exercising becomes the habit — and there’s a chance you won’t start again for weeks.

The rule that saved more habits than motivation ever did
On ownership

“Take responsibility for your mistakes.”

Not because it’s noble. Because blame is a room with no windows, and responsibility is the door.

Javier
On perspective

“A lot of bad things happened to me, and I’m happy now that they happened.”

They changed the trajectory of my life. I couldn’t see it at the time. I can see it now.

Hindsight, doing its thing
The present
Quit. Don’t quit. Noodles. Don’t noodles. You are too concerned with what was and what will be. Yesterday is history · tomorrow is a mystery · today is a gift

That is why it is called the present.

On seeing

“People’s minds are changed through observation.”

Not arguments. Not captions. Watch something long enough and it will teach you how to see it.

A note to self, aged 19
The five-minute rule

If it takes less than five minutes, it takes less than five minutes.

This is the rule I break most and the one I return to fastest. The email, the dish, the message you’ve been avoiding — it is almost never the task that is heavy. It is the carrying of it.

Do it now, then forget it
On finishing

Done is a place. Perfect is a direction.

Don’t be a perfectionist when perfection isn’t required. Most things need to exist before they need to be beautiful.
